✨ What This Collection Is

awesome-game-mashups is a carefully maintained list of "mashup" games — projects where someone took a game and rebuilt it inside another game's world, engine, or tools. Some of them are playful experiments. Some are serious technical achievements that took years. All of them are fun to look at, and many of them you can actually play yourself.

You do not need to be a programmer to enjoy this. Everything is described in plain language, grouped by how the mashup was made and how hard it is to try, and labelled so you know what you are getting before you spend time on it.

🧩 What You Will Find Inside

The list is organised around the different ways a game can end up inside another game. Here is a plain-English tour of the main categories:

  • Cross-game mechanics — one game's rules, weapons, physics, or characters dropped into another game's world. Think of a shooter's movement and guns suddenly working in a building game.
  • Engine and runtime mashups — projects where two different game engines or runtimes are made to talk to each other, so a game can be played somewhere it was never designed to run.
  • Playable ports — full recreations you can launch and play for yourself, often built from the ground up by fans.
  • Decompilation and recompilation projects — groups who carefully rebuilt classic games so they run natively on modern computers with better performance and modern screens.
  • Verified viral projects — the famous ones you have probably seen in a short video clip, checked so you know they are real, playable, and safe to look at rather than just a clever edit.
  • Mods and total conversions — existing games transformed so completely that they feel like an entirely different title.

Every entry includes a short summary, what makes it interesting, and where the project lives so you can explore it further.
